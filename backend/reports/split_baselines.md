## Baseline Macro F1: random split vs events the model never saw

| Model | Random split | Events it never saw |
|---|---|---|
| Count features only (hashtags, URLs, mentions, !, emoji) | 0.508 | 0.457 |
| TF-IDF on cleaned text | 0.784 | 0.541 |
| TF-IDF + count features | 0.775 | 0.541 |

"Events it never saw" is the mean over 4 PHEME events with at least 100 threads and at least
10 false threads, each held out in turn (fit on the other events plus PolitiFact train rows).

Setup: word 1-2-gram TF-IDF (`min_df=2`, `sublinear_tf=True`) on `text_clean`; count features on `text_raw`,
standardised; `LogisticRegression(class_weight='balanced', max_iter=3000)`.

### Finding

TF-IDF scores 0.784 Macro F1 on the random split but 0.541 on events it never saw.
A simple model partly learns the news event rather than misinformation itself. Notebooks 04 to 07 must report
both numbers, which is why `event` stays in every split CSV. Count features add nothing to TF-IDF.

The exploratory figures in the plan (0.927 random, 0.735 unseen events) were not reproduced: their setup was not
recorded, and under the earlier rumour labels the TF-IDF configurations tried (word and character n-grams, C of 1 and
10, with and without class weights, with and without dedup) scored 0.81-0.86 on the random split and 0.52-0.60 on
unseen events.

## Label semantics

`label=1` means **false**: a PHEME rumour annotated false, or a PolitiFact fake title. `label=0` is a true rumour, a
non-rumour or a PolitiFact real title. Unverified rumours and the `prince-toronto` and `ebola-essien` events are not in
the splits (see the label audit). Many PolitiFact "real" titles are page names rather than claims (for example
"Transcripts"); dedup removes their repeats, but single copies remain as a known dataset limitation.
